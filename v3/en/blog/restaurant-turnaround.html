Turnarounds

Full room, empty till

Shani Ahiel · 6 min read

One of the most frustrating situations in this industry: the room is full, guests are happy, and at the end of the month there is no money. The answer is almost always hiding in three numbers.

I founded and ran Yafo, a restaurant and bar with weekly events. In a business like that, a small difference in costs quickly becomes a large difference at the bottom line. These are the numbers I look at first when I step into a business that needs a turnaround.

Food cost

Not just what the produce costs, but how much ends up in the bin, in generosity that was never priced, and in dishes that do not earn their place on the menu. Costed recipes, regular stock counts and disciplined purchasing are the foundation.

At Shishi we worked directly with farmers. When a supplier was short of an ingredient, the dish simply came off the menu that week. That flexibility costs money if you do not plan for it, and saves money when you do.

Labour cost

In most restaurants this is the largest line, and the first to slip. I compare the rota against real demand by hour and day, and look at how roles are split and what the team does in quiet hours. The goal is not to cut people, but to put the right people in the right hours.

A great team is not a cost. An unplanned one is.

A weekly profit and loss

A report that arrives once a quarter tells you what happened. A weekly one lets you change what will happen. A simple, consistent structure (revenue, food cost, labour and fixed costs) reviewed every week with management, is the fastest way to regain control.

What to do tomorrow morning

  • Re-cost your five best-selling dishes.
  • Compare last week’s rota with sales by hour.
  • Set a weekly numbers meeting, same day, same time.

And if the numbers still do not add up, that is exactly where a diagnosis begins.

Shani Ahiel founded and ran Yafo and Shishi in Berlin, and led the food & beverage operation at Selina Berlin.
