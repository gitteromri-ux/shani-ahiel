Outsourced food & beverage: when it is right for a hotel

Shani Ahiel · 5 min read

Hotels know how to sell rooms. Restaurants, bars and events are a different business, with their own rhythm, people and risks. That is what outsourced food & beverage is for.

At Selina Berlin I led the hotel’s entire food & beverage operation against measurable targets, in a partnership model. It is the model I recommend to many hotels: whoever runs the restaurant is a true partner in the outcome, not just another supplier.

Signs it is time

  • The restaurant is open, but local guests rarely come.
  • Hotel managers spend more time on kitchen problems than with guests.
  • You have no clear weekly view of food cost and labour cost.
  • Event enquiries arrive, but no one knows how to sell, price and produce them.

Why a partnership model

When compensation is tied to agreed targets, everyone looks at the same numbers. The hotel gets a restaurant run like an independent business; the operator gets a real reason to succeed. What matters most is defining the measures up front: what is measured, how often, and what happens when a number moves.

Good outsourcing does not take responsibility away from the hotel. It gives it to someone who knows how to carry it.

What the agreement should cover

  • Clear targets: revenue, costs and guest satisfaction.
  • Ownership of team, purchasing, maintenance and events.
  • Full transparency: regular reporting and weekly working sessions.
  • A concept that fits the hotel’s brand and draws an outside crowd.

If the restaurant in your hotel is an obligation rather than an asset, it may be waiting for a different model. A short introductory call is enough to see whether outsourcing is right for you.

Shani Ahiel founded and ran Yafo and Shishi in Berlin, and led the food & beverage operation at Selina Berlin.
