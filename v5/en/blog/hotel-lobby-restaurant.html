How to turn a hotel lobby into a real restaurant

Shani Ahiel · 6 min read

The lobby is the first thing a guest sees, and often the last thing a hotel thinks of as a business. At Amano I saw it could be far more: a restaurant with a name, an audience of its own, and a reason to come even if you are not staying upstairs.

Most lobbies are planned around check-in: armchairs, coffee, perhaps a small bar that opens in the evening. They serve people already in the building, but bring no one in from the street. At Amano the goal was the opposite: a real restaurant, not a dining corner inside a hotel.

Concept comes first

A lobby restaurant only works when it has an identity of its own: a menu, music, lighting and service people tell their friends about. We did not adapt Yafo to the hotel. We brought its spirit, the Israeli food, the energy and the warm hospitality, and the hotel benefited.

Guests don’t come to a lobby. They come to a place.

Design the flow, not just the furniture

  • A clear separation between reception and seating, so diners never feel they are eating in a corridor.
  • A bar you can see from the entrance, because it is the invitation to come in.
  • Lighting that shifts from morning to evening, because the same room must work as a café and as a restaurant.

A restaurant team, not a hotel team

Reception and restaurant service are two different professions, and the difference is felt in the first second: are you welcomed as a guest or as a room number? Much of the work is building a team that understands hospitality, and agreeing clear responsibilities with hotel management: who owns what, how event bookings work, what happens at peak times.

Measure it like a restaurant

If the lobby is a business, measure it as one: revenue, food cost, labour cost, and how many diners come from outside the hotel. When those numbers are on the table every week, decisions about the menu, the rota and events become much easier.

A lobby that becomes a restaurant brings the hotel additional revenue and the reputation of a living place. It does not always require a major renovation. It requires a concept, a team and a method.
