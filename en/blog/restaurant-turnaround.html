Full room, empty till: the three numbers that restore control

Shani Ahiel · 8 min read

One of the most frustrating situations in this industry: the room is full, guests are happy, and at the end of the month there is no money. The answer is almost always hiding in three numbers.

I built Yafo from scratch, and today it is a 200-seat restaurant and bar with weekly events. At that scale, a small difference in costs very quickly becomes a large difference at the bottom line. These are the numbers I look at first when I step into a business that needs a turnaround.

1. Food cost

Not just what the produce costs, but how much of it ends up in the bin, in generosity that was never priced, and in dishes that don’t earn their place on the menu. Menu engineering, costed recipes, weekly stock counts and disciplined purchasing are the foundation.

At Shishi we worked directly with farmers. When one of our regular suppliers was short of an ingredient, the dish simply came off the menu that week. That flexibility costs money if you don’t plan for it – and saves money when you do.

2. Labour cost

The largest line in most restaurants, and the first to slip out of control. I compare the rota against real demand by hour and day, look at how roles are split, and what the team does in quiet hours. The goal is not to cut people – it is to put the right people in the right hours.

A great team is not a cost. An unplanned one is.

3. A weekly P&L

A P&L that arrives once a quarter tells you what happened. A weekly one lets you change what will happen. A simple, consistent structure – revenue, food cost, labour, fixed costs – reviewed every week with management is the fastest way to regain control.

What to do tomorrow morning

  • Re-cost your five best-selling dishes.
  • Compare last week’s rota against sales by hour.
  • Set a weekly numbers meeting – same day, same time.

And if the numbers still don’t add up, that is exactly where a diagnosis begins.
